S13 FIG. S13 · The May 8, 2026 UAP drop

143 of 144.
Still no aliens.

On May 8, 2026 the U.S. Department of War launched war.gov/UFO and posted Tranche 1 of the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. Roughly 17 pages of newly declassified text plus 162 multimedia files. Apollo 17 lunar transcripts, FBI infrared captures, an INDOPACOM football-shaped object near Japan. Officially, zero confirmations of extraterrestrial origin. The files are "unresolved" because the data is insufficient, not because the data is alien.

What the release actually contains, what it does not, and how the numbers stack up against 79 years of U.S. government UAP investigations. Four hand-coded charts: the Tranche 1 inventory, the resolution rate across every major investigation since 1947, the 60-times rise in reports since destigmatization, and the public-belief gap that government transparency has not closed since Gallup started asking in 1996.

Lead with the moon. The Apollo 17 photo is three bright dots above the lunar terrain. The transcript reads "There's a whole bunch of big ones on my window. It looks like the Fourth of July out of Ron's window." On May 8, 2026, the U.S. government opened a public website where anyone, without a security clearance, can download Apollo astronaut transcripts about objects on the Moon.

The website is war.gov/UFO. The program is the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. It was ordered by Trump Truth Social directive on February 19, 2026, and signed off at the Cabinet level by Hegseth at the renamed Department of War, Gabbard at ODNI, Patel at FBI, and Isaacman at NASA. Tranche 1 is roughly 17 pages of newly declassified text plus about 162 documents, photos, and videos. Items span June 1947 through 2026.

The official verdict on aliens is the same as it has been for every prior U.S. government UAP investigation. None confirmed. The DoW page is explicit that files are released before analysis and that "unresolved" means insufficient data, not anomalous origin. AARO's former director Sean Kirkpatrick called the rollout a "shiny object" timed against the Iran war. Trump, in Phoenix on April 17, told the audience "I don't know if [aliens are] real or not." The release is structurally unprecedented and informationally underwhelming, in the same breath.

§ III FIG. S13.2 · The 79-year resolution rate

14,500-plus cases. Zero confirmed.

Across every major U.S. government UAP investigation since 1947, cases get sorted into three buckets. Resolved means a prosaic explanation was found: balloon, drone, bird, satellite, aircraft. Unresolved means the data was insufficient to make a determination. True anomaly means the case "merits further analysis" because the observed behavior cannot be explained with current physics or known objects. There is, technically, a fourth bucket called confirmed extraterrestrial. It has never been used.

The four buckets, in plain language

Resolved. A prosaic explanation accounts for the observation. AARO FY24 breaks resolved cases into balloons (70%), unmanned aerial systems (16%), birds (8%), satellites (4%), and aircraft (2%).

Unresolved. The case lacks the sensor data, witness corroboration, or follow-up to make a determination. It is not a confirmed anomaly. It is simply unfinished.

True anomaly. AARO's term for cases whose reported behavior cannot be explained with known objects or current physics. FY24 flagged 21. Director Jon Kosloski has said publicly he cannot explain them with his physics or IC background.

Confirmed extraterrestrial. A case where the origin has been formally determined to be non-terrestrial. Across Project Blue Book, ODNI, AARO, and now PURSUE, this bucket has held zero cases for 79 years.

§ IV FIG. S13.3 · The 60-times rise

Eight reports a year. Then four hundred.

The shape of the volume curve is the story. Project Blue Book averaged about 573 reports per year through the 1950s and 60s. The 1970-2007 period had no formal U.S. government UAP investigation at all. By the time the ODNI baseline arrived in 2021, only 144 reports had been logged across 17 years, an average of 8 per year. After destigmatization, AARO's most recent in-period count is 485 new reports in 13 months, a 60-times rise. The story is not aliens. The story is that sensors got better, airspace got more cluttered, and the stigma of reporting collapsed.

What changed in 2020

Three things. First, the Pentagon officially released FLIR1, Gimbal, and GoFast in April 2020, ending the question of whether Navy pilots were imagining things. Second, NDAA FY22 created a formal reporting mandate (50 U.S.C. section 3373) that gave service members a legitimate channel without career risk. Third, AARO stood up in July 2022 with both classified and unclassified reporting workflows. The combination collapsed the stigma that had silenced reports for fifty years.

The volume curve is not evidence of more aliens. It is evidence of more reports. AARO FY24 resolves 70% of cases as balloons or unmanned aerial systems. The signal is mostly drone proliferation and improved sensor coverage, not a contact event.

§ V FIG. S13.4 · The belief gap

Government says no. The public stopped listening in 1996.

The official answer has not changed in 79 years. Public belief has changed enormously. Gallup tracked "some UFOs are alien spacecraft" from 20% in 1996 to 41% in 2021. YouGov 2025 finds 56% of Americans say aliens definitely or probably exist, and 47% say aliens have visited Earth at some point. Belief that the government is concealing what it knows runs above 60%; only 11% say the government has been transparent. Government transparency events (the 2017 NYT story, the 2021 ODNI assessment, the 2023 Grusch testimony, the 2024 AARO Historical Record, today's PURSUE drop) have not closed the gap. The gap may be the story.

The demographics

Belief skews higher in the West (Gallup 2019: 40% in the West vs 27% in the Midwest). Twenty percent of Westerners say they have personally seen a UFO; 24% nationwide per YouGov 2025. Men lead women in most surveys. Lower-income and non-college respondents lead in some Gallup waves, though YouGov 2025 shows that gap closing.

Personal sightings predict belief but not as strongly as media consumption. Surveys conducted within six months of a major disclosure event (2017 NYT, 2021 ODNI, 2023 Grusch testimony) show 5 to 8 point belief bumps that decay back to baseline within 18 months. The PURSUE release falls inside this same disclosure bump pattern.

§ VI Methodology, sources, caveats

Method

Five sections, four hand-coded SVG charts, one editorial frame. The Tranche 1 inventory is taken directly from the war.gov/UFO carousel and the May 8, 2026 DoW press release. Resolution rates are taken from the source agency reports (Project Blue Book, ODNI 2021, AARO 2022/2023/FY24). Polling is taken from Gallup, YouGov, and Pew published methodologies.

Charts are hand-coded SVG with no charting library. Tooltips are vanilla JavaScript, ~140 lines total. Page weight is well under one megabyte. No tracking beyond the platform-level Cloudflare Web Analytics.

Sources

Government records: ODNI Preliminary Assessment (Jun 25, 2021), AARO 2022 Annual Report (Jan 2023), AARO Historical Record Vol. I (Mar 8, 2024), AARO FY24 Annual Report (Nov 14, 2024). Project Blue Book Fact Sheet via National Archives.

Caveats

Distinguish documents from claims about documents. The DoW page is explicit that files are released before analysis. Several outlets have characterized images more dramatically than the captions warrant. Tier-1 sources for the visualization are the war.gov/UFO captions themselves. The ArtThreat "FBI imagery from New Year Eve 1999" attribution does not appear in the official war.gov captions and may be a misattribution.

The Immaculate Constellation program is alleged, not confirmed. DoD spokesperson Sue Gough denied any record of an SAP by that name. It does not appear in PURSUE Tranche 1 and is not in the inventory map.

The Tic-Tac speed of 24,000 mph or "Mach 20+" is derived from radar drop-time inferences (28,000 ft to 50 ft in ~0.78 s) often cited by Kevin Day. The Pentagon has not officially endorsed any speed figure. Treat as eyewitness/operator derived, not an official measurement.

The Iran war is the dominant news context. NPR/PBS/Marist late April 2026 showed >60% disapproval of military action in Iran. AARO's former director Sean Kirkpatrick publicly characterized the timing of the May 8 release as a deliberate distraction. The chart deck acknowledges this without endorsing it.

What this lab is not

Not a believer's argument. The data does not support the conclusion that aliens have visited Earth, and this lab does not pretend otherwise.

Not a debunker's argument. Twenty-one cases AARO calls "true anomalies" is not zero. The honest framing is that the government has cases it cannot explain, and that absence of explanation is not the same as confirmation of origin.

Not an authored take on whether PURSUE constitutes meaningful disclosure or political distraction. Both characterizations are documented and cited. The reader decides. The data is just the data.

The single most surprising fact in the May 8, 2026 release is not in any of the photographs. It is the disclaimer at the top of the page: "Many of the materials have not yet been analyzed for resolution of any anomalies."

The U.S. government has, for the first time in 79 years, published files publicly before reaching a conclusion about them. Not because the conclusion would be uncomfortable, but because the conclusion does not yet exist. That is structurally unprecedented. It is also a small first tranche of what officials describe as a recurring rolling release.

Whether PURSUE constitutes meaningful disclosure or political distraction depends on what arrives in Tranche 2, Tranche 3, Tranche N. If subsequent releases keep coming, contain analysis, and eventually include the materials whistleblowers say are still being held back, this is a turning point. If they do not, it is a press release. The interface is permanent. The verdict is not.

FAQ

What is PURSUE and what was released on May 8, 2026?

PURSUE is the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. Tranche 1, launched May 8, 2026 at war.gov/UFO, contains roughly 17 newly declassified text pages plus 162 documents, photos, and videos drawn from NASA, FBI, DoD, ODNI, AARO, DOE, and the State Department. Items span June 1947 through 2026.

Did the May 8 release confirm extraterrestrial life?

No. The Department of War explicitly notes the files are "unresolved" due to "a lack of sufficient data," not because they have been deemed alien. Across 1,652+ catalogued UAP reports through October 2024, AARO has discovered no evidence of extraterrestrial beings, activity, or technology. The official language is "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena" and "unresolved cases."

How does PURSUE compare to past UAP disclosures?

Quantitatively smaller than AARO's existing catalog (1,652 cases), but qualitatively unprecedented. It is the first time the U.S. government has packaged FBI, NASA, DoD, and ODNI UAP holdings into a single citizen-facing portal that requires no security clearance. Prior milestones: Project Blue Book (12,618 sightings, 701 unexplained, 1947-1969), the June 2021 ODNI Preliminary Assessment (144 reports, 143 unresolved), the AARO Historical Record Vol. I (March 2024, no empirical evidence of off-world technology), and the AARO FY24 Annual Report (November 2024, 757 new reports, 21 "true anomalies").

What is the Immaculate Constellation report?

An alleged Special Access Program named in a whistleblower report surfaced by journalist Michael Shellenberger on November 13, 2024 during a House "Exposing the Truth" hearing. The report claims a multi-decade UAP retrieval and reverse-engineering program. DoD spokesperson Sue Gough denied any record of an SAP by that name. The program is alleged, not confirmed, and does not appear in PURSUE Tranche 1.

What do Americans actually believe about UFOs?

Belief is high and rising. Gallup tracked "some UFOs are alien spacecraft" from 20% in 1996 to 41% in 2021. YouGov 2025 found 56% of Americans say aliens definitely or probably exist; 47% say aliens have visited Earth at some point. Over 60% believe the government is concealing UFO information; only 11% say government has been transparent. Twenty-four percent of Americans report having personally seen a UFO. Belief skews higher in the West than the Midwest.
